# Methods library

This file contains original summaries of public methods approved for persona research and communication adaptation. Use the methods as tools, not as universal truths or guaranteed conversion formulas.

## 1. Value Proposition Canvas - Strategyzer
Public source: https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-value-proposition-canvas

Permitted use:
- describe one customer segment at a time;
- identify jobs the customer is trying to get done;
- identify pains encountered around those jobs;
- identify gains or outcomes the customer values;
- test assumed fit against evidence instead of treating the canvas as proof.

Operational rule for persona building:
Map only evidence-backed jobs, pains, and gains. Rank by relevance to the communication use case, not by how dramatic they sound. Keep unsupported items as hypotheses.

Limits:
The framework structures understanding; it does not prove that a job, pain, or gain is prevalent in a population. Evidence still has to come from research.

## 2. Jobs to Be Done - Christensen and coauthors
Public source: https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done

Permitted use:
- focus on the progress a person is trying to make in a specific situation;
- avoid overrelying on static demographic profiles;
- investigate why a person chooses, changes, delays, or rejects a solution;
- consider functional, social, and emotional dimensions when evidence supports them.

Operational rule:
Write jobs as contextual progress, not vague aspirations. Example pattern: "When [situation], the avatar needs to [progress] so that [desired outcome]."

Limits:
Do not turn JTBD into mind-reading. The job must be inferred from interviews, behavior, credible research, or other evidence.

## 3. EAST - Behavioural Insights Team
Public source: https://www.bi.team/east-tool/methodology/

EAST organizes practical behavioral design around Easy, Attractive, Social, and Timely.

Permitted use in writing:
- Easy: reduce unnecessary cognitive or procedural friction;
- Attractive: make relevant information salient and aligned with real value;
- Social: use truthful social norms, trusted messengers, or commitments when supported;
- Timely: communicate at moments when the information is relevant and actionable.

Limits:
BIT explicitly presents EAST as a practical framework, not a comprehensive map of behavioral science. Do not fabricate social norms, urgency, or outcomes.

## 4. Ethical influence - Robert Cialdini / APA / Influence at Work
Public sources:
- https://www.apa.org/news/podcasts/speaking-of-psychology/persuasion
- https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/

Public descriptions identify principles such as reciprocity, liking, scarcity, social proof, authority, consistency, and unity. The public material emphasizes ethical use rather than manipulation.

Permitted use:
- reciprocity only when genuine value is being provided;
- authority only when credentials or expertise are real and relevant;
- social proof only with real and properly scoped evidence;
- scarcity only when the limitation is real;
- consistency or commitment without coercive escalation;
- liking and unity without exploiting protected or sensitive identity traits.

Limits:
Do not represent these principles as deterministic. Never fabricate the factual trigger required by the principle.

## 5. CDC Clear Communication Index
Public sources:
- https://www.cdc.gov/ccindex/tool/description-examples-parta.html
- https://www.cdc.gov/ccindex/tool/faq.html
- https://www.cdc.gov/ccindex/tool/how-to-use.html

Useful principles:
- define one main message;
- make the main message easy to find;
- state a call to action when appropriate;
- use active voice and words familiar to the primary audience;
- organize content in chunks with headings and lists;
- summarize the most important information early;
- communicate what is known and not known;
- explain behavioral recommendations and how to perform them;
- handle numbers and risk deliberately.

Permitted use beyond health:
Use the communication-design principles for professional and public materials, while recognizing that health-specific recommendations may not apply outside health contexts.

Limits:
The Index is an assessment and design tool. It does not replace direct audience research.

## 6. Nielsen Norman Group web-writing research
Public sources:
- https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/
- https://www.nngroup.com/articles/applying-writing-guidelines-web-pages/

Useful principles:
- users often scan web content rather than reading linearly;
- concise writing improves usability;
- descriptive headings and chunking improve scanning;
- objective, low-hype writing can outperform promotional "marketese" in usability studies.

Permitted use:
Adapt structure for fast comprehension, especially for busy professional avatars and digital pages.

Limits:
The cited studies are older web-usability research. Treat them as enduring design evidence, not as a current universal performance benchmark for every channel.

## 7. Public Formula de Lancamento material - Erico Rocha
Public sources include:
- https://ericorocha.com.br/2020/08/04/o-que-sao-lancamentos/
- https://ericorocha.com.br/2020/07/07/gatilho-da-antecipacao/
- https://ericorocha.com.br/2020/09/29/gatilho-da-escassez/
- https://ericorocha.com.br/2020/03/17/gatilho-da-prova-social/

Permitted use as practitioner technique reference:
- launch sequences can build attention before an offer;
- a seed launch is publicly described as a way to validate an offer through an initial sale;
- anticipation is described as building attention before opening an offer;
- scarcity should not be based on a false limitation;
- proof and social proof are distinct concepts in the public material.

Operational rule:
Use these concepts only when they fit the avatar, channel, and truthful facts. Separate practitioner method from independently established behavioral evidence.

Limits:
Commercial outcomes and performance claims on the provider's site are provider claims unless independently verified. Do not reproduce paid course material or imply that the public summaries are the full proprietary methodology.

## 8. Cross-method writing rules
When adapting text for a persona:
1. preserve factual and technical source content;
2. make the audience's relevant job or decision context visible;
3. state the main message early;
4. reduce unnecessary cognitive load;
5. translate jargon only when translation does not change technical meaning;
6. use concrete consequences and examples relevant to the avatar;
7. anticipate evidence-backed objections;
8. use persuasion only when the underlying claim is truthful and supported;
9. avoid hype when credibility is a major trust criterion;
10. make next steps specific when a next step is appropriate.
